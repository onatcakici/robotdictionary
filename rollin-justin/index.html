    Humanoids

    Rollin' Justin

    n., humanoid. German Aerospace Center DLR, Germany, 2008. Research platform.

    Wheeled humanoid from DLR with torque controlled arms and hands, commanded from the International Space Station in orbital telerobotics experiments.

    Measures and facts
    MakerGerman Aerospace Center DLR
    CountryGermany
    First shown2008
    StatusResearch platform
    Height1.91 m
    Mass200 kg
    Payload20 kg
    Top speed2 m/s (7.2 km/h)
    Degrees of freedom51
    Runtime1 h
    ActuationTorque controlled electric joints with tendon driven hands
    SensorsTorque sensors in nearly all joints, two stereo cameras, five depth cameras, inertial units

    Rollin' Justin combines the Justin humanoid upper body, first built in 2006, with a four legged wheeled base whose legs extend and retract, giving 51 degrees of freedom, a height of 1.91 m and a mass around 200 kg. Nearly every joint measures torque, so a hierarchical whole body controller can grip objects softly, absorb collisions and juggle several tasks at once, while the robot carries about 20 kg and moves at up to 2 m/s. In the METERON SUPVIS Justin experiments, astronauts aboard the space station commanded it through a tablet interface to service a mock Mars solar array on the ground in Germany.

    Of note

    • Commanded from the International Space Station in the METERON SUPVIS Justin experiments
    • Torque sensing in nearly every joint for compliant whole body control
    • Introduced in 2009
    • Developed at the DLR institute in Wessling, Germany
    • Operates wirelessly
