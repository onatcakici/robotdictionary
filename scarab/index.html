    Scarab

    n., rover. Carnegie Mellon University, United States, 2008. Research platform.

    Lunar prospecting testbed rover with a body that kneels to press a drill into the ground, tested on Hawaiian volcanic soil.

    Measures and facts
    MakerCarnegie Mellon University
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2008
    StatusResearch platform
    ActuationFour wheel drive on a body that kneels and changes its footprint
    PowerBattery electric drive designed for very low power operation
    SensorsLaser scanner for driving in darkness, drill and volatiles payload
    AutonomyTeleoperated with autonomous navigation in darkness

    Scarab was designed to prospect for water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters, so it runs on modest power and can drive in complete darkness using a laser scanner rather than cameras. Its four wheel chassis can kneel and change its footprint, using the whole vehicle mass to give the drill the reaction force needed to cut a metre into regolith. It was field tested on the slopes of Mauna Kea in Hawaii carrying NASA's RESOLVE volatiles prospecting payload, drilling and heating soil to detect water.

    Of note

    • Kneels so its own weight provides drilling reaction force
    • Drilled with NASA's RESOLVE payload during Mauna Kea field tests
