    Humanoids

    ARMAR-III

    n., humanoid. Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, Germany, 2006. Research platform.

    Kitchen oriented humanoid from Karlsruhe with 43 degrees of freedom on a holonomic wheeled base, used for household manipulation research.

    Measures and facts
    MakerKarlsruhe Institute of Technology
    CountryGermany
    First shown2006
    StatusResearch platform
    Degrees of freedom43
    ActuationElectric motors with harmonic drive gears and tendon driven hands
    SensorsStereo foveated camera head, microphones, force and torque sensing in the arms
    AutonomyTask level planning with learning from human demonstration

    ARMAR-III pairs a human like torso, head, arms and five fingered hands with a holonomic wheeled platform, giving 43 degrees of freedom in total. Its head carries stereo cameras with foveated vision, microphones and inertial sensing, while the arms combine position, velocity and force torque sensing for compliant manipulation. Two units, built in 2006 and 2008, anchored years of work in the German collaborative research centre on humanoid robots, loading dishwashers, fetching drinks and learning tasks from human demonstration.

    Of note

    • Central platform of the German collaborative research centre on humanoid robots
    • Worked for years in a full sized mock kitchen
