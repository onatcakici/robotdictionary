    Humanoids

    Robonaut 1

    Also known as R1

    n., humanoid. NASA Johnson Space Center with DARPA, United States, 2000. Retired.

    NASA's first dexterous humanoid torso, built with DARPA to test whether a teleoperated robot could handle astronaut tools and handrails.

    Measures and facts
    MakerNASA Johnson Space Center with DARPA
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2000
    StatusRetired
    ActuationElectric brushless motors with tendon driven fingers
    SensorsStereo camera head with position, force and tactile sensing through the arms and hands
    AutonomyTelepresence operation with some autonomous grasping
    Priceapprox. $5,900

    Robonaut 1 paired two arms and five fingered hands with a stereo camera head on a torso sized to an astronaut's work envelope, and was driven through telepresence gloves and a head mounted display. Two units, R1A and R1B, were tested with a zero gravity leg that grips handrail sockets, on a Segway based Robotic Mobility Platform in 2003 and on the four wheeled Centaur 1 chassis in 2006. None flew in space, but its hands and control software led directly to Robonaut 2.

    Of note

    • First NASA humanoid built to use the same tools as astronauts
    • Tested on Segway based and four wheeled Centaur mobility platforms
