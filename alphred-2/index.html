    Quadrupeds

    ALPHRED 2

    n., quadruped. UCLA Robotics and Mechanisms Laboratory, United States, 2019. Research platform.

    Radially symmetric four limbed robot from UCLA that walks in any direction, uses two limbs as arms and can roll on its feet.

    Measures and facts
    MakerUCLA Robotics and Mechanisms Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2019
    StatusResearch platform
    Degrees of freedom12
    ActuationCustom back drivable BEAR electric actuators, three per limb
    PowerOnboard battery
    SensorsInertial measurement unit, joint sensing
    AutonomyOnboard torque control with several locomotion modes

    ALPHRED 2 places four identical three joint limbs around a central body with no fixed front, so it can walk sideways, stand on two limbs and manipulate with the other two, or hop and roll. Its custom BEAR actuators are back drivable and torque controlled, giving the compliance needed for dynamic contact with the ground and with objects. Dennis Hong's group built it to explore multi modal locomotion for tasks such as package delivery, where a robot must both travel and handle loads.

    Of note

    • Radially symmetric layout lets it walk in any direction without turning
    • Can convert two of its four limbs into arms for manipulation
