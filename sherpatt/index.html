    Rovers

    SherpaTT

    n., rover. DFKI Robotics Innovation Center, Germany, 2016. Research platform.

    Hybrid wheeled leg rover with actively articulated suspension, field tested in the Utah desert and in Morocco for Mars analogue work.

    Measures and facts
    MakerDFKI Robotics Innovation Center
    CountryGermany
    First shown2016
    StatusResearch platform
    Mass150 kg
    Top speed0.7 m/s (2.5 km/h)
    ActuationFour legs with driven and steered wheels and active ground adaption
    PowerLithium polymer battery packs with autonomous switching
    SensorsRotating lidar, laser rangefinder, high resolution camera, wheel force torque sensors
    AutonomyAutonomous navigation with active suspension control

    SherpaTT has four legs, each ending in a driven and steered wheel with five degrees of freedom, so the body stays level and the footprint can grow from one metre square to well over two metres. Force torque sensors in every wheel let it adapt to loose slopes, and it can walk short distances where wheels alone would dig in. DFKI ran it for weeks in Utah's Mars analogue terrain in 2016 and later in Morocco, covering long autonomous traverses and cooperating with other robots.

    Of note

    • Active suspension keeps the body level on rough slopes
    • Completed multi week Mars analogue field campaigns in Utah and Morocco
