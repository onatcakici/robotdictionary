    Roomba

    n., wheeled machine. iRobot, United States, 2002. In production.

    Disc shaped domestic vacuum robot that cleans floors on its own and returns to a dock, the product that created the home robot market.

    Measures and facts
    MakeriRobot
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2002
    StatusIn production
    Height0.09 m
    Length0.34 m
    ActuationTwo drive wheels with a caster, side brush and counter rotating brushes or rubber rollers
    PowerRechargeable battery with a self charging home base
    SensorsBumper, cliff and dirt detection sensors, with an upward camera on later models
    AutonomyRandom coverage on early models, visual mapping and systematic rows on later ones
    Units soldover 40 million

    A Roomba is a low disc about 340 mm across with two drive wheels, a side brush and either bristle brushes or rubber rollers. Early generations covered a room by bouncing semi randomly with bump, cliff and dirt sensors, following the view that simple behaviours beat expensive mapping. From the sixth generation the robots added an upward camera and floor tracking sensor to build a map and clean in tidy rows, plus self emptying bases on higher models. Tens of millions have been sold for homes worldwide.

    The Roomba made autonomous robots a household category: over forty million sold, an ecosystem of competitors, and a cultural fixture ridden by cats. iRobot's founders came from MIT's insect robot lab, and it shows in the bump and turn instincts.

    Of note

    • Launched in September 2002 and helped create the consumer robot market
    • IRobot filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in December 2025 and was acquired the following year
    • Amazon's proposed acquisition of iRobot was abandoned in 2024
