    Wheeled machines

    Shakey

    n., wheeled machine. SRI International, United States, 1966. Retired.

    The first mobile robot able to reason about its own actions, a wheeled machine that pushed blocks around laboratory rooms under program control.

    Measures and facts
    MakerSRI International
    CountryUnited States
    First shown1966
    StatusRetired
    ActuationWheeled cart with a drive base and a camera mast
    PowerOnboard batteries with a radio link to a mainframe computer
    SensorsTelevision camera, sonar range finders and bump detectors
    AutonomyOff board planning with the STRIPS planner over a radio link

    Shakey was built at Stanford Research Institute between 1966 and 1972 as a tall wheeled cart carrying a television camera, sonar range finders and bump detectors, linked by radio to a mainframe. Given a command such as pushing a block off a platform, it broke the task into steps using the STRIPS planner, then drove, found a ramp and pushed the block. Work on the project produced the A star search algorithm and the Hough transform, both still in use. The robot is retired and displayed at the Computer History Museum in California.

    Shakey, built at SRI between 1966 and 1972, was the first robot to plan its own actions, and the research around it produced the A* search algorithm and the Hough transform, foundations of computing far beyond robotics.

    Of note

    • Work on Shakey produced the A star search algorithm and the Hough transform
    • Inducted into the Robot Hall of Fame in 2004 and marked with an IEEE Milestone
    • On display at the Computer History Museum in Mountain View, California
