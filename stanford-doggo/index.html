    Stanford Doggo

    n., quadruped. Stanford Student Robotics, United States, 2019. Retired.

    Open source quasi direct drive quadruped built by students for a few thousand dollars that set a vertical jumping record.

    Measures and facts
    MakerStanford Student Robotics
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2019
    StatusRetired
    Degrees of freedom8
    ActuationEight quasi direct drive brushless motors, two per leg
    PowerOnboard lithium polymer battery
    SensorsInertial measurement unit, motor position sensing
    AutonomyOpen loop gait state machine on a microcontroller

    Doggo has four two joint legs, each driven by a pair of coaxially mounted brushless motors with almost no gear reduction, so leg forces are controlled directly through motor current. A microcontroller and off the shelf motor drivers run a state machine that produces trotting, bounding, pronking and jumping from open loop leg trajectories. Weighing under five kilograms, it jumped repeatedly to about a metre, the highest vertical jumping agility measured for a robot at the time, and its full design files were published for anyone to build.

    Of note

    • Highest vertical jumping agility recorded for a legged robot when published in 2019
    • Complete mechanical and electrical design released as open source
