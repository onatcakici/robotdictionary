    Humanoids

    Spring Flamingo

    n., humanoid. MIT Leg Laboratory, United States, 1996. Retired.

    Planar walking biped at MIT that pioneered series elastic actuators and force controlled walking on flat ground and shallow slopes.

    Measures and facts
    MakerMIT Leg Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown1996
    StatusRetired
    Height0.9 m
    Mass13.5 kg
    Degrees of freedom6
    ActuationSeries elastic actuators with cable drives from body mounted motors
    AutonomyIntuitive force based walking control, boom constrained to the sagittal plane

    Spring Flamingo is 90 cm tall, weighs about 13.5 kg and has six force controlled joints, an actuated hip, knee and ankle on each leg, with motors in the body driving them through cables. Jerry Pratt built it around series elastic actuators, which place a spring between motor and load so joint force can be measured and controlled precisely, and used simple intuitive control laws instead of tracking precomputed trajectories. The robot walked continuous laps around a boom and its control ideas carried into later humanoid work at IHMC.

    Of note

    • Early demonstration of series elastic actuation for legged robots
    • Control approach carried forward into later IHMC humanoid work
