    Mini Cheetah

    n., quadruped. MIT Biomimetic Robotics Laboratory, United States, 2019. Research platform.

    Nine kilogram electric quadruped built from modular actuators, the first four legged robot shown performing a backflip.

    Measures and facts
    MakerMIT Biomimetic Robotics Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2019
    StatusResearch platform
    Mass9 kg
    Top speed2.45 m/s (8.8 km/h)
    Degrees of freedom12
    ActuationTwelve modular proprioceptive brushless actuators
    PowerOnboard lithium polymer battery
    SensorsInertial measurement unit, joint encoders
    AutonomyOnboard model predictive and learned controllers
    Featfirst quadruped backflip

    Mini Cheetah uses twelve identical low cost modular actuators derived from drone motors, which makes it cheap to build and quick to repair after hard falls. Its controllers came from the Cheetah 3 line and were later replaced in research work by learned policies, and it trots at about 2.45 metres per second and recovers from being kicked. MIT built a small fleet and lent units to other universities, and the design was widely copied, including by commercial makers in China.

    MIT's Mini Cheetah was the first quadruped to backflip, and its open design philosophy spawned a family of clones that seeded China's quadruped industry.

    Of note

    • First quadruped robot to perform a backflip
    • Its open documentation and modular actuator design were widely copied in low cost quadrupeds
