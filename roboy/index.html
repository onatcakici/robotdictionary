    Humanoids

    Roboy

    n., humanoid. University of Zurich Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, Switzerland, 2013. Research platform.

    Tendon driven humanoid built in about nine months by a Zurich led consortium to copy human muscle and tendon actuation in a child sized body.

    Measures and facts
    MakerUniversity of Zurich Artificial Intelligence Laboratory
    CountrySwitzerland
    First shown2013
    StatusResearch platform
    Height1.42 m
    Degrees of freedom28
    ActuationAbout 48 brushless motors driving tendons with force sensing
    SensorsCameras, microphone, tendon force and joint position sensing
    AutonomySpeech and face recognition driving scripted interaction

    Roboy is about 1.42 m tall with 28 degrees of freedom moved by roughly 48 electric motors that pull tendons across the joints, imitating how muscles act on human bones. Much of the skeleton is 3D printed, and the design, electronics and software were published openly with parts contributed by more than a dozen partner organisations. The University of Zurich AI Laboratory presented it in 2013 and the project later continued at the Technical University of Munich as a platform for musculoskeletal control and human robot interaction.

    Of note

    • Built in roughly nine months by a consortium of universities and companies
    • Tendon driven musculoskeletal design on a largely 3D printed skeleton
