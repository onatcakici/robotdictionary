    Dante I

    Also known as Dante

    n., rover. Carnegie Mellon University, United States, 1992. Retired.

    Eight legged tethered walking robot sent into the crater of Mount Erebus in Antarctica, which failed a few metres into its descent.

    Measures and facts
    MakerCarnegie Mellon University
    CountryUnited States
    First shown1992
    StatusRetired
    ActuationEight legs in two frames, moving one frame at a time
    PowerSupplied through a tether from the crater rim
    SensorsCameras and laser rangefinder
    AutonomyTeleoperated under supervisory control

    Dante I was built by the Carnegie Mellon Field Robotics Center to show that a tethered walking machine could enter a volcanic crater too dangerous for people. It moved as a frame walker, sliding one frame of legs past the other, with power and communications carried on a tether from the crater rim. The tether failed shortly after it started down into the crater of Mount Erebus at the end of 1992, ending the descent after only a few metres.

    Of note

    • First attempt to send a walking robot into an active volcano
    • Tether failure ended the Mount Erebus descent
