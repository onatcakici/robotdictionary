    Robots of Canada

    32 entries built in Canada, grouped by section.

    Humanoids

    Archie n., humanoid. University of Manitoba, Canada, 2004. Teen sized humanoid robot over 120 cm tall, developed from 2004 by TU Wien in Austria and the University of Manitoba in Canada. Research platform.

    HR6 n., humanoid. Dr Robot, Canada. Humanoid robot model produced by Dr Robot, a robotics company based in Canada.

    Meccanoid n., humanoid. Spin Master, Canada, 2015. Buildable humanoid robot kit from Meccano that learns movements by recording poses its owner moves it through. Discontinued.

    Phoenix n., humanoid. Sanctuary AI, Canada, 2023. Hydraulically actuated general purpose humanoid built around highly dexterous hands, trialled in a Canadian retail store and with automotive supplier Magna. Prototype.

    Quadrupeds

    Scout II n., quadruped. McGill University, Canada, 2000. Minimalist running quadruped from McGill with only one actuated joint per leg and passive springy lower legs. Retired.

    Zoomer n., quadruped. Spin Master, Canada, 2014. Interactive toy robot dog that responded to voice commands and learned tricks. Discontinued.

    Wheeled machines

    CHiP n., wheeled machine. WowWee, Canada. Toy robot dog that rolls on spherical wheels and follows its owner using a wristband. Discontinued.

    Dingo n., wheeled machine. Clearpath Robotics, Canada, 2020. An indoor research base light enough for a lab bench. In production.

    Husky n., wheeled machine. Clearpath Robotics, Canada, 2010. The default outdoor research base of a thousand robotics papers. In production.

    Husky A300 n., wheeled machine. Clearpath Robotics, Canada, 2024. The modernised Husky, weatherproofed and faster for field research. In production.

    Jackal n., wheeled machine. Clearpath Robotics, Canada, 2015. A weatherproof compact research rover for field robotics. In production.

    MiP n., wheeled machine. WowWee, Canada, 2014. Self balancing toy robot that rides on two wheels, carries a small tray and responds to hand gestures. Discontinued.

    Neo n., wheeled machine. Avidbots, Canada, 2024. Self driving floor scrubbing machine for large indoor sites that maps a building once and then repeats cleaning routes without an operator. In production.

    Neo 2 n., wheeled machine. Avidbots, Canada, 2019. A self driving floor scrubber mapping airports and malls each night. In production.

    OTTO 1500 n., wheeled machine. OTTO Motors, Canada, 2015. Heavy duty self driving vehicle for factories that moves pallets and large assemblies weighing up to 1,900 kg without floor guidance. In production.

    TurtleBot 4 n., wheeled machine. Clearpath and Open Robotics, Canada, 2022. The standard issue teaching robot of the ROS curriculum. In production.

    Warthog n., wheeled machine. Clearpath Robotics, Canada, 2016. An amphibious heavyweight research vehicle for mines, farms and shorelines. In production.

    Arms and workcells

    Canadarm n., robot arm. SPAR Aerospace, Canada, 1981. Family of robotic arms built by SPAR Aerospace of Canada that Space Shuttle orbiters carried to release, move and capture payloads in orbit. Retired.

    Canadarm2 n., robot arm. MDA Space and FRE Composites, Canada, 2001. Robotic arm on the International Space Station.

    Canadarm2 n., robot arm. MDA, Canada, 2001. Robotic arm of the International Space Station, built in Canada, that moves end over end between fixtures and captures visiting cargo ships. In service.

    Canadarm3 n., workcell. MDA, Canada. Robotic system planned for the Gateway lunar space station, combining a large arm, a small dexterous arm and tools, built by MDA for the Canadian Space Agency. In development.

    Dextre n., workcell. MDA Space, Canada, 2008. Robotic telemanipulator with two arms on the International Space Station, built by MDA Space to handle repair work that would otherwise call for spacewalking astronauts. In service.

    Gen3 n., robot arm. Kinova, Canada. Ultralight seven axis arm from Kinova used in research labs and on mobile robots. In production.

    JACO n., robot arm. Kinova, Canada. Lightweight arm mounted on a power wheelchair so people with limited upper body mobility can eat, drink and open doors. In production.

    Meca500 n., robot arm. Mecademic, Canada. Very small six axis industrial arm from Mecademic, sized for laboratory automation and electronics assembly. In production.

    teaBOT n., workcell. teaBOT, Canada. Robotic kiosk that blends and brews loose leaf tea to order, placed in offices and showrooms as a workplace amenity.

    Medical and surgical

    KINARM n., medical robot. BKIN Technologies, Canada. Interactive robotic device that lets researchers measure how the brain handles sensing, movement and thinking through upper limb tasks, made by BKIN Technologies of Kingston, Ontario.

    Revolve n., medical robot. Revolve Surgical, Canada. Compact surgical robot designed to fit operating rooms outside hospitals, where many abdominal procedures take place.

    Trexo n., exoskeleton. Trexo Robotics, Canada. Powered walking exoskeleton that lets children with conditions such as cerebral palsy take steps, often for the first time.

    Underwater and marine

    ROPOS n., underwater robot. International Submarine Engineering, Canada. Remotely operated underwater vehicle used mainly for science, built in Vancouver by International Submarine Engineering and later purchased by the Canadian federal government. In service.

    Other forms

    hitchBOT n., robot. McMaster University, Canada, 2013. Hitchhiking robot from Canada, made by academics at McMaster and Ryerson universities, that crossed three countries on rides from strangers before it was destroyed. Lost.

    Nuum n., snake robot. Nuum Robotics, Canada. Snake like robot that travels inside machinery to inspect and maintain it.
