    Robots of Japan

    97 entries built in Japan, grouped by section.

    Humanoids

    Actroid n., android. Kokoro, Japan, 2003. Highly lifelike android created at Osaka University and produced by Kokoro, usually given the appearance of a young Japanese woman. In production.

    Affetto n., humanoid. University of Osaka, Japan, 2012. Research robot developed at the University of Osaka in Japan, first built in 2012. Research platform.

    Aiko Chihira n., android. Toshiba, Japan, 2015. Humanoid android from Toshiba that greeted shoppers as a receptionist at a Mitsukoshi department store in Tokyo. Retired.

    Alter 3 n., humanoid. Osaka University and mixi, Japan, 2019. An android artist with a neural network nervous system; conducted an opera. Research platform.

    ASIMO n., humanoid. Honda, Japan, 2000. The world's most famous walking robot for two decades; retired to museum duty in 2022. Retired.

    ASRA C1 n., humanoid. Sony AI, Japan, 2023. Sony's dual armed cooking research robot developed with Carnegie Mellon. Research platform.

    Buddharoid n., humanoid. Kyoto University, Japan. Japanese robot monk created by Kyoto University researchers with technology company partners to give Buddhist spiritual guidance, unveiled at a temple in Kyoto.

    CUE n., humanoid. Toyota, Japan, 2019. A basketball shooting humanoid holding a Guinness record for consecutive free throws. Research platform.

    E2-DR n., humanoid. Honda, Japan, 2017. Honda's disaster response humanoid that shimmies through gaps and climbs wet ladders. Research platform.

    Enon n., humanoid. Fujitsu, Japan, 2005. Humanoid service robot from Fujitsu Frontech and Fujitsu Laboratories that performs assistance tasks autonomously, drawing on a network connection.

    Erica n., android. Ishiguro Laboratories, Japan, 2015. A conversational android of studied realism; once hired as a television news anchor. Research platform.

    Geminoid HI-1 n., humanoid. ATR Intelligent Robotics and Communication Laboratories with Osaka University, Japan, 2006. Teleoperated android copy of researcher Hiroshi Ishiguro, built to study how human presence transfers through a remotely controlled likeness. Research platform.

    HOAP n., humanoid. Fujitsu Automation, Japan. Series of humanoid robots made by Fujitsu Automation in Japan and offered as an open architecture platform.

    HRP-2 n., humanoid. AIST and Kawada, Japan, 2002. Japan's national research humanoid, star of a hundred university labs. Retired.

    HRP-2P n., humanoid. Kawada Technologies, Japan. Humanoid robot model in the HRP series, built by the Japanese company Kawada Technologies.

    HRP-3 n., humanoid. Kawada Technologies and National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology, Japan, 2007. Japanese humanoid robot introduced in 2007 by Kawada Technologies in partnership with the National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology.

    HRP-4 n., humanoid. AIST and Kawada Industries, Japan, 2010. Slim humanoid from Japan's national research institute AIST and Kawada, lighter than earlier HRP machines. Research platform.

    HRP-4C n., humanoid. AIST, Japan, 2009. A singing, catwalk walking gynoid built to human proportions for entertainment research. Retired.

    HRP-5P n., humanoid. AIST, Japan, 2018. A heavy labour research humanoid that hangs drywall and handles full size panels. Research platform.

    ibuki n., android. Ishiguro Laboratories, Japan, 2018. A child android on wheels, built to study affinity with young humanoids. Research platform.

    Kaleido n., humanoid. Kawasaki, Japan, 2017. Kawasaki's rugged full size humanoid, iterated yearly toward heavy field work. Research platform.

    Kengoro n., humanoid. University of Tokyo, Japan, 2016. A musculoskeletal humanoid that sweats through its frame to cool its motors while doing push ups. Research platform.

    Kenshiro n., humanoid. University of Tokyo JSK Laboratory, Japan, 2012. Musculoskeletal humanoid scaled to an average Japanese schoolboy and driven by about 160 tendon actuators arranged like human muscles. Research platform.

    KHR-1 n., humanoid. KO PROPO, Japan, 2004. Programmable two legged humanoid robot introduced by the Japanese firm Kondo Kagaku in 2004, when it ranked among the lowest priced robots of its type.

    KHR-3HV n., humanoid. Kondo Kagaku, Japan, 2009. Hobby humanoid kit with seventeen servo joints, sold to enthusiasts and schools and long a standard machine in Japanese robot fighting leagues. In production.

    Kirobo n., humanoid. University of Tokyo with Toyota, Dentsu and Robo Garage for JAXA, Japan, 2013. Small talking humanoid flown to the International Space Station in 2013 to test conversation between an astronaut and a robot in orbit. Retired.

    Kobian n., humanoid. Waseda University, Japan. Humanoid robot developed at Waseda University in Japan that can show emotional expressions and was made to perform greetings suited to particular cultures. Research platform.

    Kodomoroid n., humanoid. Ishiguro Laboratories, Japan, 2014. A child faced android newsreader on permanent duty at Tokyo's Miraikan museum. Research platform.

    NEXTAGE n., humanoid. Kawada Robotics, Japan, 2011. Dual arm humanoid torso sold for precision cell production, working at benches alongside people in electronics and precision assembly plants. In production.

    Otonaroid n., android. Hiroshi Ishiguro Laboratories, Japan, 2014. Adult female android by Hiroshi Ishiguro, shown with Kodomoroid at Tokyo's Miraikan science museum in 2014. Retired.

    P3 n., humanoid. Honda, Japan, 1997. Honda's first fully self contained walking humanoid, ancestor of ASIMO. Retired.

    PALRO n., humanoid. Fuji Soft, Japan, 2010. Small humanoid communication robot used to lead games and exercises in Japanese care homes for the elderly.

    PINO n., humanoid. Kitano Symbiotic Systems Project, JST ERATO, Japan, 2001. Small open design humanoid from a Japanese research programme, built from low cost parts and published so other laboratories could copy it. Retired.

    QRIO n., humanoid. Sony, Japan, 2003. Sony's running, dancing small humanoid, cancelled before sale but never forgotten. Retired.

    Repliee Q2 n., android. Osaka University and Kokoro, Japan, 2005. Female android from Hiroshi Ishiguro's laboratory, built with air actuators under silicone skin to study how people react to lifelike machines. Retired.

    RIBA n., humanoid. RIKEN and Tokai Rubber Industries, Japan, 2009. Bear faced care robot that could lift a person from a bed into a wheelchair, the forerunner of ROBEAR. Retired.

    ROBEAR n., humanoid. RIKEN, Japan, 2015. A bear faced nursing robot strong enough to lift patients gently from bed to wheelchair. Research platform.

    RoBoHoN n., humanoid. Sharp, Japan, 2016. Palm size walking humanoid that is also a working mobile phone, sold to Japanese consumers and businesses across several model revisions. In service.

    Ropid n., humanoid. Robo Garage, Japan. Small humanoid robot created by Tomotaka Takahashi of Robo Garage at Kyoto University.

    S-One n., humanoid. SCHAFT, Japan, 2013. The DRC Trials winner from a Google acquired startup later shut down; its walking was years ahead. Retired.

    SDR-4X n., humanoid. Sony Group, Japan. Humanoid robot developed in Japan by Sony; its SDR designation stands for Sony Dream Robot.

    T-HR3 n., humanoid. Toyota, Japan, 2017. A teleoperated avatar humanoid that mirrors its pilot through force feedback exoskeleton controls. Research platform.

    Telenoid n., humanoid. Ishiguro Laboratories, Japan, 2010. A minimal, ghost like telepresence android reduced to the essence of a person. Research platform.

    Torobo n., humanoid. Tokyo Robotics, Japan, 2015. A research grade torque controlled humanoid sold to manipulation labs. In production.

    Toyota Partner Robot n., humanoid. Toyota, Japan, 2005. Series of humanoid robots from Toyota that debuted by playing drums and trumpets at Expo 2005 in Aichi, Japan.

    Twendy-One n., humanoid. Waseda University, Japan, 2007. A wheeled care humanoid with soft hands able to hold an egg or support a person standing. Research platform.

    WABIAN-2R n., humanoid. Waseda University, Humanoid Robotics Institute, Japan, 2006. Full size biped used at Waseda to study human like walking, carrying a movable waist and trunk plus a passive toe joint. Research platform.

    WABOT-1 n., humanoid. Waseda University, Kato Laboratory, Japan, 1973. Full scale anthropomorphic robot built at Waseda University that walked on two legs, gripped objects and held simple spoken exchanges. Retired.

    WABOT-2 n., humanoid. Waseda University, Kato Laboratory, Japan, 1984. Keyboard playing humanoid from Waseda University that read printed sheet music with a camera eye and performed on an electronic organ. Retired.

    Wakamaru n., humanoid. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Japan, 2005. Yellow domestic robot from Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, built to interact with people in a natural way and offered only to Japanese households from 2005.

    Quadrupeds

    aibo ERS-1000 n., quadruped. Sony, Japan, 2018. Sony's revival of its robot dog, a twenty two axis home companion with camera based recognition and cloud connected behaviour. Discontinued.

    AIBO ERS-110 n., quadruped. Sony, Japan, 1999. Sony's first consumer robot dog, an eighteen joint autonomous quadruped sold as an entertainment pet rather than a research machine. Discontinued.

    Banryu n., quadruped. Sanyo, Japan. Robot product model developed in Japan and manufactured by the electronics company Sanyo.

    Bex n., quadruped. Kawasaki, Japan, 2022. A rideable robot ibex that carries a person on its back and kneels on wheels. Announced, not yet in service.

    CORLEO n., quadruped. Kawasaki, Japan, 2025. A hydrogen powered rideable quadruped concept steered by the rider's body. Announced, not yet in service.

    iDog n., quadruped. Sega Fave, Japan, 2005. Robot dog toy from Sega that flashes its lights and dances along to music played into it from an iPod or other MP3 player.

    KUMO-I n., quadruped. Tokyo Institute of Technology, Japan, 1976. Spider inspired research quadruped by Shigeo Hirose and Yoji Umetani, the first machine in the long walking robot programme at Tokyo Institute of Technology. Retired.

    Poo-Chi n., quadruped. Sega Toys, Japan, 2000. Low cost robotic puppy that walked on four legs, sold worldwide as a cheap toy alternative to expensive entertainment robots. Discontinued.

    Tekken-II n., quadruped. University of Electro-Communications, Japan, 2002. Small electric quadruped used to test neural oscillator gait control with reflexes on irregular outdoor ground. Retired.

    TITAN VIII n., quadruped. Tokyo Institute of Technology, Japan, 1996. Compact wire driven quadruped from the Hirose laboratory, built as a general purpose walking platform for research and teaching. Retired.

    TITAN XI n., quadruped. Tokyo Institute of Technology, Japan, 2004. Seven tonne hydraulic quadruped built to drill anchor holes on steep slopes, among the largest walking machines ever completed. Retired.

    Wheeled machines

    Honda Miimo n., wheeled machine. Honda, Japan. Battery powered robotic lawn mower model produced by the Japanese company Honda.

    HOSPI n., wheeled machine. Panasonic, Japan, 2004. Autonomous hospital delivery robot that moves medicines, specimens and records between departments around the clock in locked trays. In service.

    HSR n., wheeled machine. Toyota, Japan, 2015. Toyota's Human Support Robot, a one armed home helper standard in RoboCup@Home. Research platform.

    Innovative Autonomous Haulage Vehicle n., wheeled machine. Komatsu, Japan, 2016. Purpose built cabless mining haul truck with four wheel drive and four wheel steering that runs equally well in both directions without an operator station. Prototype.

    LOVOT n., wheeled machine. GROOVE X, Japan, 2019. A warm bodied companion robot engineered solely to be loved. In production.

    MEISTeR n., wheeled machine. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Japan. Remotely controlled maintenance robot from Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, built for work inside the damaged Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant.

    NICOBO n., wheeled machine. Panasonic, Japan, 2021. A shy tail wagging cushion robot that speaks in half formed words. In production.

    OMRON LD-250 n., wheeled machine. OMRON, Japan, 2019. Industrial autonomous mobile robot that carries 250 kg of parts between machines and stores using laser mapping instead of floor tape or magnets. In production.

    Quince n., wheeled machine. Chiba Institute of Technology and Tohoku University, Japan, 2011. Tracked Japanese rescue robot that climbed stairs inside the damaged Fukushima Daiichi reactor buildings to measure radiation and take samples. Retired.

    T-52 Enryu n., wheeled machine. Tmsuk, Japan. Rescue robot produced by the Japanese robotics company Tmsuk for use in rescue work.

    Whiz n., wheeled machine. SoftBank Robotics, Japan, 2019. A vacuum sweeper trained by walking it once, then set loose on office carpets. In production.

    Arms and workcells

    COBOTTA n., robot arm. Denso Wave, Japan. Portable six axis collaborative arm from Denso light enough to be carried and set up on a desk. In production.

    CRX-10iA n., robot arm. FANUC, Japan, 2019. Lightweight collaborative arm from FANUC programmed by dragging it by hand or with a tablet interface. In production.

    duAro n., robot arm. Kawasaki Heavy Industries, Japan, 2015. Two armed SCARA style robot from Kawasaki on a wheeled base, sized to take the place of one worker on an assembly line. In production.

    JEMRMS n., robot arm. JAXA, Japan, 2008. Robotic arm system of the Japanese Kibo module on the International Space Station, used to move experiments onto the module's exposed platform. In service.

    LR Mate 200iD n., robot arm. FANUC, Japan. Compact six axis arm from FANUC, one of the most widely installed small industrial robots. In production.

    M-2000iA n., robot arm. FANUC, Japan. Very large six axis arm from FANUC that can lift car bodies and other loads weighing more than two tonnes. In production.

    Motoman HC10 n., robot arm. Yaskawa, Japan. Collaborative arm in the Yaskawa Motoman range with hand guided teaching and pinch point protection. In production.

    Rovers

    LEV-1 n., rover. JAXA, Japan, 2024. Small hopping robot released from JAXA's SLIM lander in January 2024 that relayed pictures taken by its companion SORA-Q back to Earth. Mission completed.

    LUPEX rover n., rover. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Japan. Tracked lunar rover from Mitsubishi Heavy Industries of Japan for the Lunar Polar Exploration Mission, weighing 350 kg and measuring 2 m long. In development.

    MINERVA-II1 n., rover. JAXA, Japan, 2018. Pair of small rovers released by Hayabusa2 onto asteroid Ryugu in 2018, the first rovers to move across the surface of an asteroid, which they did by hopping. Mission completed.

    SORA-Q n., rover. JAXA with Tomy, Sony and Doshisha University, Japan, 2023. Transformable ball shaped lunar robot the size of a baseball that photographed the tipped over SLIM lander in January 2024. Mission completed.

    Sorato n., rover. ispace and Team Hakuto, Japan, 2017. Four kilogram carbon fibre rover built for the Google Lunar XPRIZE by Team Hakuto, completed and tested but never launched. Retired.

    Yaoki n., rover. Dymon, Japan, 2025. Palm sized Japanese commercial rover with two wide wheels that can right itself, flown on the IM-2 mission but never deployed. Lost.

    Medical and surgical

    HAL n., exoskeleton. University of Tsukuba and Cyberdyne Inc., Japan, 2004. Powered exoskeleton suit with a soft body, known in full as the Hybrid Assistive Limb, built by Japan's University of Tsukuba and Cyberdyne to boost its wearers' physical abilities.

    Underwater and marine

    ABISMO n., underwater robot. Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology, Japan. Japanese deep sea remotely operated vehicle built by the Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology as the replacement for Kaikō, which was lost in 2003. In service.

    Other forms

    ACM III n., robot. Tokyo Institute of Technology, Japan, 1972. Snake robot built by Shigeo Hirose, among the first machines to crawl by undulating its body like a real snake. Retired.

    Int-Ball n., robot. Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency and University of Tokyo, Japan, 2016. Ball shaped camera robot from the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency and the University of Tokyo that flies inside the ISS Kibō module, filming tasks to save crew time. In service.

    Keepon n., robot. Hideki Kozima and Marek Michalowski, Japan, 2003. Small yellow snowman shaped robot built for research on social interaction with autistic children, later sold as the dancing toy My Keepon.

    Kirobo Mini n., robot. Toyota, Japan. Compact robot developed by the Japanese company Toyota under the Kirobo Mini name.

    Moflin n., robot. Casio, Japan, 2024. Furry palm sized companion robot that develops its own character as it responds to touch and voice. In production.

    Murata Boy and Murata Girl n., robot. Murata Manufacturing, Japan. Pair of self balancing robots built by Japanese electronic components maker Murata Manufacturing to demonstrate its products and attract publicity.

    NeCoRo n., robot. Omron, Japan. Robot built in the form of an animal and sold as a product by the Japanese company Omron.

    PaPeRo n., robot. NEC Corporation, Japan, 1997. Personal robot developed by the Japanese firm NEC, known for its endearing appearance and facial recognition, with work starting in 1997 on the R100 prototype.

    Qoobo n., robot. Yukai Engineering, Japan, 2018. Tailed cushion robot that wags its tail when stroked, made as a pet substitute. In production.

    SORA-Q Flagship Model n., robot. Tomy, Japan, 2023. Radio controlled mobile toy robot released in 2023 by the Japanese toy company Tomy as the flagship model bearing the SORA-Q name.
