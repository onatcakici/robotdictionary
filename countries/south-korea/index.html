    Robots of South Korea

    29 entries built in South Korea, grouped by section.

    Humanoids

    DAL-e n., humanoid. Hyundai, South Korea, 2021. A wheeled customer service humanoid greeting visitors in Hyundai showrooms. In pilot deployments.

    DARwIn-OP n., humanoid. ROBOTIS, South Korea, 2010. The open platform mini humanoid that carried university RoboCup teams for a decade. Retired.

    DRC-Hubo+ n., humanoid. KAIST with Rainbow Robotics, South Korea, 2015. Transformable KAIST humanoid that won the 2015 DARPA Robotics Challenge Finals by switching between walking and rolling on knee wheels. Research platform.

    EveR n., android. Korea University of Science and Technology, South Korea, 2003. Line of female android robots developed by South Korean researchers at the Korea Institute of Industrial Technology and first shown to the public in Seoul in 2003. Research platform.

    Gabi n., humanoid. South Korea, 2026. Humanoid robot monk presented by the Jogye Order of Korean Buddhism in 2026 as a way to bring the faith closer to younger Koreans.

    HUBO n., humanoid. KAIST Humanoid Robot Research Center, South Korea, 2005. Third generation KAIST biped with five fingered hands and independently moving eyes that walked at 1.25 km/h, danced and gripped objects. Research platform.

    MAHRU n., humanoid. Korea Institute of Science and Technology, South Korea. Humanoid robot developed by the Korea Institute of Science and Technology that draws its artificial intelligence from networks, created together with the related robot AHRA. Research platform.

    Mahru-Z n., humanoid. Korea Institute of Science and Technology, South Korea, 2010. KIST humanoid built for household chores, using three dimensional vision to recognise objects and operate appliances with its own hands and feet. Research platform.

    MUSA n., humanoid. Seoul National University, South Korea, 2005. Kendo fighting robot completed in July 2005 by the Manufacturing and Mechatronics Lab of Seoul National University under Professor Young-Bong Bang.

    RB-Y1 n., humanoid. Rainbow Robotics, South Korea, 2024. Wheeled two armed humanoid manipulator sold as a development platform, notable for a six axis body that raises and leans the torso while driving. In production.

    ROBOTIS OP3 n., humanoid. ROBOTIS, South Korea, 2017. Small open platform biped sold worldwide for education and research, built from the maker's Dynamixel smart servos and used in humanoid soccer leagues. In production.

    Quadrupeds

    Genibo n., quadruped. Dasarobot, South Korea, 2006. Korean entertainment robot dog styled as a bull terrier, sold as a home pet with voice command control. Discontinued.

    HOUND n., quadruped. KAIST, South Korea, 2023. A sprinting quadruped holding a Guinness 100 metre record for its class. Research platform.

    RaiBo n., quadruped. KAIST, South Korea, 2022. Learning controlled quadruped from KAIST able to run across a sandy beach, terrain that defeats most legged robots. Research platform.

    Raptor n., quadruped. KAIST, South Korea, 2014. Two legged running robot designed at KAIST in 2014, modelled on the velociraptor and able to run at up to 46 km/h. Research platform.

    RBQ-10 n., quadruped. Rainbow Robotics, South Korea, 2023. A Korean industrial quadruped for patrol and payload work. In production.

    RBQ-3 n., quadruped. Rainbow Robotics, South Korea. Electric quadruped from the Korean maker of the HUBO humanoids, sold as a platform for research and field inspection. In production.

    Wheeled machines

    Ballie n., wheeled machine. Samsung, South Korea, 2025. A rolling home companion ball with a built in projector, launched after six years of teasers. In production.

    CLOi ServeBot n., wheeled machine. LG, South Korea, 2020. LG's shelf carrying restaurant and building courier line. In production.

    CRX10 n., wheeled machine. CNRobot, South Korea, 2011. Self navigating mobile robot platform from South Korean company CNRobot, intended for teaching, training and research and first shown in May 2011.

    Dilly n., wheeled machine. Woowa Brothers, South Korea, 2019. Korea's food delivery robot rolling through Seoul apartment complexes. In pilot deployments.

    Jet Bot AI+ n., wheeled machine. Samsung, South Korea, 2021. Robot vacuum from Samsung that combines lidar with a 3D sensor and object recognition to avoid small obstacles. In production.

    LG CLOi GuideBot n., wheeled machine. LG Electronics, South Korea, 2017. Airport and lobby guide robot with a large touch screen that answers questions in several languages and escorts visitors to their destination. In production.

    Navibot n., wheeled machine. Samsung, South Korea, 2010. Robot vacuum from Samsung that worked out its position by photographing the ceiling with an upward facing camera. Discontinued.

    Q9 n., wheeled machine. LG, South Korea, 2025. LG's two legged wheeled home agent that emotes through a screen face. Announced, not yet in service.

    TurtleBot3 n., wheeled machine. ROBOTIS, South Korea, 2017. Small open source research and teaching robot with a spinning laser scanner, used in university courses and labs to learn mapping and navigation. In production.

    Arms and workcells

    M1013 n., robot arm. Doosan Robotics, South Korea. Collaborative arm from Doosan Robotics with torque sensors in all six joints. In production.

    Other forms

    Avadin n., robot. Wonderful Platform, South Korea. Care robot for older people developed by the South Korean company Wonderful Platform, also known by the name Dasom.

    Samsung SGR-A1 n., robot. Samsung Techwin and Korea University, South Korea. Autonomous sentry gun developed by Samsung Techwin and Korea University to help South Korean soldiers guard the Korean Demilitarized Zone.
