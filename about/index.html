    About the dictionary

    Robot Dictionary is a reference of the world's robots: one entry per machine, written to be read in a minute and checked in two.

    What is in it

    The present edition holds 1,002 entries from 644 makers in 43 countries, arranged in eight sections: humanoids, quadrupeds, wheeled machines, arms and workcells, rovers, medical and surgical robots, underwater and marine robots, and other forms. A robot earns an entry when it was built and shown in public, or announced with enough published detail to describe it.

    How an entry is made

    Each entry gives the robot's maker, country, the year it was first shown, its present status, a short definition and, where they are published, its measures. Longer entries add a history and notes. Every entry lists its sources at the foot of the page: makers' own publications, Wikidata, Wikipedia, company directories, and press or research papers where those are the best record.

    Figures are copied as published and rounded only where the source gives a range. Where sources disagree, the maker's own figure is preferred and the disagreement is noted. Nothing in an entry is guessed; when a fact is unknown it is left out.

    Revised every day

    A revision runs every morning. It refreshes facts drawn from Wikidata, picks up robots that have newly appeared in the public record, cuts new pictures, sets the day's Robodle, selects the robot of the day and rebuilds every page. The changes of each day are listed under Recently added and in the RSS feed.

    Corrections and additions

    Write to contact@robotdictionary.com with the entry and a source, or edit the entry directly on GitHub, where every entry is a small text file. Makers are welcome to send official figures and photographs.

    Open data

    The whole dictionary can be downloaded as JSON or CSV and reused under CC BY 4.0 with credit to Robot Dictionary. Text quoted from Wikipedia is marked on the page and keeps its own CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.

    Pictures

    Pictures come from Wikimedia Commons under free licences and are shown in reduced size to keep pages fast. Each one is credited in the sources of its entry and on the picture credits page. Entries without a suitable free picture are shown without one.

    News

    Each morning the dictionary also collects robotics headlines from publishers and links them to the robots they mention. They appear on the front page, on the robots' own entries and under News.

    Robodle and the chart

    Robodle is a daily game drawn from the dictionary: one robot a day, shown only as a shadow, to be named in eight guesses. The evolution of robots puts every dated entry on a single chart, so the whole history can be seen at once. Both are rebuilt every morning with the rest of the site.
