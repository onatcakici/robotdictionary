    Wheeled machines

    RHINO

    n., wheeled machine. University of Bonn, Germany, 1997. Retired.

    Research tour guide robot that led museum visitors for six days in 1997, among the first robots to work unattended in a public crowd.

    Measures and facts
    MakerUniversity of Bonn
    CountryGermany
    First shown1997
    StatusRetired
    Top speed0.8 m/s (2.9 km/h)
    ActuationDifferential drive research base carrying three onboard computers
    PowerBattery powered research base
    Sensors24 ultrasonic transducers, two laser range finders covering 360 degrees, infrared and touch panels
    AutonomyProbabilistic localisation with map based planning and collision avoidance in crowds

    RHINO was a wheeled research base fitted with a ring of 24 ultrasonic transducers, two laser range finders covering 360 degrees, infrared sensors and touch panels. Probabilistic localisation and map based planning let it keep track of itself and pick paths through moving crowds, with three onboard computers and two workstations doing the work. It guided visitors around exhibits at the Deutsches Museum Bonn, explaining each stop, and remote users could also send it to exhibits over the web. It travelled 18.6 km in about 47 hours of operation and is now retired.

    Of note

    • Guided more than 2,000 museum visitors during a six day deployment in May 1997
    • Travelled 18.6 km in about 47 hours with only six minor collisions
    • Also served about 2,000 remote visitors who directed it over the web
