    Section VIII

    Other forms

    Snakes, climbers, companions, spheres and machines that fit no other shelf. 131 entries.

    #

    2-XL n., toy robot. United States, 1978. Talking educational toy robot sold by Mego from 1978 to 1981 and by Tiger Electronics from 1992 to 1995, regarded as the first smart toy. Discontinued.

    A

    ACM III n., snake robot. Tokyo Institute of Technology, Japan, 1972. Snake robot built by Shigeo Hirose, among the first machines to crawl by undulating its body like a real snake. Retired.

    Adelbrecht n., robot. Martin Spanjaard. Ball shaped robot that could talk and interact with people, designed by Martin Spanjaard. Retired.

    Aerones n., robot. Aerones, Latvia. Rope suspended robotic system that climbs wind turbine blades to clean, inspect, test lightning conductors and repair leading edges.

    AISoy1 n., robot. AISoy Robotics, Spain. Consumer pet robot from Spain's AISoy Robotics, counted among the first with emotional learning, whose decisions weigh logical and emotional factors rather than fixed replies.

    Ajeeb n., automaton. 1868. Fake chess automaton built by cabinet maker Charles Hooper, which appeared to play on its own while a skilled human player hidden inside made the moves.

    Alice n., robot. École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne, Switzerland, 1998. Tiny cube shaped mobile robot measuring 2 by 2 by 2 cm, created at the Autonomous Systems Lab of the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne. Research platform.

    Andy Warhol Robot n., animatronic figure. Andy Warhol, 1981. Animatronic figure of Andy Warhol that the artist created in 1981 to serve as a self portrait.

    Argus n., robot. United States, 1990. Robotic camera platform from MIT's City Scanning project that captured omnidirectional images tagged with their geographic position, dating from 1990.

    Astrobee n., robot. Ames Research Center, United States, 2014. System of three cube shaped free flying robots from NASA's Ames Research Center that helps astronauts with routine chores aboard the International Space Station. In service.

    Avadin n., robot. Wonderful Platform, South Korea. Care robot for older people developed by the South Korean company Wonderful Platform, also known by the name Dasom.

    Axle Robot Kit n., robot kit. True Robotics, United States. Robot kit from the American company True Robotics that students in grades 6 to 8 build for classroom learning.

    B

    BB-8 by Sphero n., toy robot. Sphero, United States, 2015. App controlled rolling toy modelled on the droid from the 2015 Star Wars film, a ball with a magnetically held head. Discontinued.

    Big Mouth Billy Bass n., animatronic figure. Gemmy Industries, United States, 1999. Singing animatronic novelty in the form of a largemouth bass, created by Gemmy Industries and widely popular from the late 1990s into the early 2000s.

    Bio-Intelligent Cyborg Insect n., robot. Biohybrid robotic system that steers living insects without surgery by using ultraviolet light, relying on their natural tendency to move away from it. Research platform.

    C

    Carl n., robot. University of Kaiserslautern, Germany. Robot in the form of a compliant robotic leg, built at the University of Kaiserslautern in Germany.

    CIMON n., robot. Airbus Commercial Aircraft, Germany. Head shaped robot with artificial intelligence, built by Airbus in Germany as a crew companion aboard the International Space Station. In service.

    CISBOT n., robot. Pipe repair robot that travels inside cast iron natural gas mains and seals their joints from within, so the pipes need not be dug up.

    Compressorhead n., animatronic figure. Germany, 2013. Robot rock cover band whose members are animatronic machines, created by Berlin based artist Frank Barnes and several collaborators.

    Cotton Candy Dan n., animatronic figure. Spirit Halloween, 2024. Animatronic clown prop shown stirring cotton candy, sold by the retailer Spirit Halloween for the 2024 Halloween season.

    Creoqode NOVA n., robot. Creoqode, United Kingdom. Educational robot model from the British company Creoqode, intended for teaching and learning about robotics.

    CubeStormer 3 n., robot. 2014. Rubik's Cube solving robot made from Lego Mindstorms and a Samsung Galaxy S4 that set a world record of 3.253 seconds in March 2014.

    CubeStormer II n., robot. Mike Dobson and David Gilday. Rubik's Cube solving robot built mostly from Lego Mindstorms parts with a Samsung Galaxy S2, made by Mike Dobson and David Gilday for ARM Holdings.

    D

    Darwin n., robot. Darwin, United States. Robot technician being built to assemble, commission and maintain data center hardware, with first deployments targeted for 2027.

    Digesting Duck n., automaton. Jacques de Vaucanson, France, 1739. Mechanical duck built by Jacques de Vaucanson that seemed to eat grain and excrete it, although the droppings were stored in advance and nothing was actually digested.

    DIY Kindle Scanner n., robot. 2012. Lego Mindstorms machine built by Peter Purgathofer that works with a computer and a Kindle reader to copy electronic books, sidestepping their copy protection.

    Don Cuco El Guapo n., robot. Universidad Autónoma de Puebla, Mexico, 1992. Piano playing robot created in 1992 by the microelectronics department of the Universidad Autónoma de Puebla in Mexico.

    E

    Edgerun n., robot. Edgerun, United States. Powered exoskeleton developed for soldiers.

    El Ajedrecista n., automaton. Leonardo Torres Quevedo, Spain, 1912. Chess automaton created by the Spanish engineer Leonardo Torres Quevedo that played out a reduced endgame with three pieces entirely on its own. Retired.

    ElliQ n., robot. Intuition Robotics. Companion robot for elderly people, developed by the company Intuition Robotics and run by artificial intelligence software.

    F

    Fingerlings n., toy robot. WowWee, 2017. Line of small robotic toy creatures from WowWee that cling to a finger and blink or blow kisses when they sense touch or sound.

    Furby n., toy robot. Hasbro, United States, 1998. Talking robot toy resembling an owl, made by Hasbro subsidiary Tiger Electronics, that became a sought after holiday gift following its October 1998 launch.

    G

    Gecko Robotics inspection robots n., robot. Gecko Robotics, United States. Fleet of magnetic wall climbing and crawling robots that scan boilers, tanks, pipelines and ship hulls for wall thickness and corrosion.

    H

    Haile n., robot. Georgia Institute of Technology, 2006. Robotic percussionist from the Georgia Institute of Technology that listens to live music through a microphone and plays its own rhythm on a real drum. Research platform.

    Hexbug n., toy robot. Spin Master, 2007. Brand of robotic insect toys from Spin Master that borrows from BEAM robotics, its first models imitating six legged arthropods. In production.

    hitchBOT n., robot. McMaster University, Canada, 2013. Hitchhiking robot from Canada, made by academics at McMaster and Ryerson universities, that crossed three countries on rides from strangers before it was destroyed. Lost.

    Hugh n., robot. Aberystwyth University, 2016. Robot librarian built as a prototype at Aberystwyth University to locate books for library users and lead them there, though it never went into use. Prototype.

    I

    I-Bot n., robot kit. Microbric, Australia, 2005. Small robot from Microbric, given out in kit form by the Adelaide Advertiser newspaper in 2005 to introduce readers to electronics and robotics.

    IceMole n., robot. FH Aachen, Germany, 2010. Autonomous, steerable ice probe developed at FH Aachen in Germany that melts and screws its way through ice for polar, glacier and space research. Research platform.

    Insbot n., robot. 2002. Robotic cockroach made in 2002 by researchers of the European LEURRE project to persuade real cockroaches to follow it, exploring natural pest control. Research platform.

    Int-Ball n., robot. Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency and University of Tokyo, Japan, 2016. Ball shaped camera robot from the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency and the University of Tokyo that flies inside the ISS Kibō module, filming tasks to save crew time. In service.

    IXI-Play n., robot. Ixiplay, United States. Android based robot companion for young children that read picture cards, mimicked the emotions on them, and ran games from its own app store. Discontinued.

    J

    Jibo n., robot. Jibo, Inc., United States, 2017. Desktop social robot conceived by Cynthia Breazeal that turned its round head toward people as it talked.

    Johns Hopkins Beast n., automaton. Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory, United States, 1960. Early self sufficient mobile machine built in the 1960s at the Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory that roamed corridors hunting for wall sockets to recharge itself. Retired.

    K

    Keepon n., robot. Hideki Kozima and Marek Michalowski, Japan, 2003. Small yellow snowman shaped robot built for research on social interaction with autistic children, later sold as the dancing toy My Keepon.

    Kilobot n., robot. Harvard University. Low cost swarm robot from Harvard University that works in groups to perform tasks beyond a single unit, such as collective transport and self assembly into shapes. Research platform.

    Kirobo Mini n., robot. Toyota, Japan. Compact robot developed by the Japanese company Toyota under the Kirobo Mini name.

    Kota the Triceratops n., animatronic figure. Hasbro and Playskool. Animatronic toy dinosaur in the form of a Triceratops that Hasbro and Playskool produced for children from 3 to 6 years old. Discontinued.

    L

    Ladybird of Szeged n., robot. University of Szeged, Hungary, 1956. Early Hungarian robot shaped like a red, black spotted ladybird, built at the University of Szeged to reproduce Pavlovian conditioned reflexes mechanically.

    Laffing Sal n., animatronic figure. United States. Animatronic figure with a loud laugh, one of several characters built to lure carnival and amusement park visitors into funhouses and dark rides in the United States.

    Land Walker n., robot. Bipedal vehicle 3.4 m tall, invented by Masaaki Nagumo, that moves at about 1.5 km/h on wheels concealed under its feet rather than truly walking.

    Laundroid n., robot. Home robot that was meant to take clothes through washing, drying, ironing, sorting and folding automatically, promoted as the first machine of its kind.

    LEGO Boost n., robot kit. The LEGO Group, Denmark, 2017. Building and coding set from LEGO with a motorised hub and a colour and distance sensor, used to make five starter robots. Discontinued.

    LEGO Education SPIKE Prime n., robot kit. The LEGO Group, Denmark, 2020. Classroom robotics set from LEGO Education built around a programmable hub with six ports for motors and sensors. In production.

    Lego Mindstorms EV3 n., robot kit. The Lego Group, Denmark, 2013. Programmable robotics kit from the Lego Group, the third generation of the Mindstorms line, announced at CES in January 2013 and succeeding the NXT.

    Lego Mindstorms NXT n., robot kit. The Lego Group, Denmark, 2006. Robot building and programming kit from Lego, launched in August 2006 as the second generation of Mindstorms after the Robotics Invention System.

    LEGO Mindstorms RCX n., robot kit. The LEGO Group, Denmark, 1998. First LEGO Mindstorms programmable brick, which brought robot building with motors and sensors to children and hobbyists. Discontinued.

    Leonardo n., robot. Massachusetts Institute of Technology, United States, 2002. Social robot standing 2.5 feet tall, built by MIT's Personal Robots Group to study interaction and collaboration between humans and robots. Research platform.

    Lewis n., robot. Washington University in St. Louis, United States. Autonomous robot photographer from the Media and Machines Laboratory at Washington University in St. Louis that roams social events taking pictures of guests. Research platform.

    Lucky the Dinosaur n., animatronic figure. United States, 2003. Roaming Disney animatronic figure of a green Segnosaurus, about 2.4 m tall, that hauls a cart decked in flowers while a handler leads it. In service.

    M

    Manifold n., robot. Manifold, United States. Drop in robotic labor from a 2026 Y Combinator company.

    Mark II n., robot. MegaBots, United States, 2015. Rideable robot controlled by its riders, made by the US company MegaBots and first shown at Maker Faire San Mateo in May 2015.

    Mechanical Turk n., automaton. Wolfgang von Kempelen, 1769. Hoax chess automaton built by Wolfgang von Kempelen whose apparent skill came from a chess master concealed inside, moving the pieces with levers and magnets. Lost.

    Mephisto n., automaton. United Kingdom, 1876. Chess playing machine passed off as an automaton, built in London by Charles Godfrey Gumpel, an Alsatian maker of artificial limbs, and first exhibited in 1878.

    MiRo n., robot. Consequential Robotics, United Kingdom. Social robot made by the British company Consequential Robotics, intended to interact and communicate with people.

    Modulus n., robot. Sirius. Domestic robot developed from 1984 by Sirius, a company founded by Massimo Giuliana, with an exterior designed by the Milan based Japanese designer Isao Hosoe.

    Moflin n., robot. Casio, Japan, 2024. Furry palm sized companion robot that develops its own character as it responds to touch and voice. In production.

    Molecubes n., robot. Cornell University. Modular robot system from Cornell University made of cubes with two rotating halves that can be joined together into many different shapes. Research platform.

    Moxie n., robot. Embodied. Companion robot developed and produced by the company Embodied and later discontinued. Discontinued.

    Muppet Mobile Lab n., animatronic figure. Walt Disney Imagineering, United States. Free roving animatronic attraction created by Walt Disney Imagineering and trialled at Disney theme parks in Anaheim, Orlando and Hong Kong. Retired.

    Murata Boy and Murata Girl n., robot. Murata Manufacturing, Japan. Pair of self balancing robots built by Japanese electronic components maker Murata Manufacturing to demonstrate its products and attract publicity.

    Musio n., robot. AKA Intelligence, 2015. Social robot from AKA Intelligence that converses with people to help them practise spoken English, recognising objects, reading facial expressions and showing expressions of its own.

    N

    Navel n., robot. navel robotics, Germany. Social robot developed by the German company navel robotics for use in care facilities.

    NeCoRo n., robot. Omron, Japan. Robot built in the form of an animal and sold as a product by the Japanese company Omron.

    Nova 5 n., robot. Autonomous search and rescue robot created by engineer brothers Fady and Sami Khaled that uses a fuzzy neural network to make sense of its surroundings and find its own way.

    Nuum n., snake robot. Nuum Robotics, Canada. Snake like robot that travels inside machinery to inspect and maintain it.

    O

    Octobot n., robot. Harvard University, 2016. Autonomous robot made of soft materials, built by a Harvard University team to show the approach could work and described in Nature in 2016. Research platform.

    Omnibot n., toy robot. Tomy. Toy robot first made by Tomy in the mid 1980s, whose name later covered a whole line of the company's robots.

    OmniTread n., snake robot. University of Michigan, United States. Segmented serpentine robot covered in moving treads on all sides, so it can push through rubble whichever way it lands. Research platform.

    Orb Swarm n., robot. 2007. Kinetic art piece made up of six partly autonomous spherical robots, built in 2007 by engineers and artists to imitate natural swarms and human dance.

    P

    PaPeRo n., robot. NEC Corporation, Japan, 1997. Personal robot developed by the Japanese firm NEC, known for its endearing appearance and facial recognition, with work starting in 1997 on the R100 prototype.

    Paro n., robot. Therapeutic robot shaped like a baby harp seal, made to soothe and draw emotional responses from patients in hospitals and care homes, much like animal therapy.

    Peacock Clock n., automaton. James Cox, 1772. Large eighteenth century automaton clock with three mechanical birds of full life size, created by the businessman James Cox.

    Phobot n., robot. University of Amsterdam, Netherlands, 2008. Robot made from Lego by students at the University of Amsterdam that mimics being afraid and conquering fear step by step, intended to help children face phobias.

    Pilot Robot Kit n., robot kit. True Robotics, United States. Educational robot kit from the American company True Robotics that pupils in grades 3 to 5 assemble themselves.

    Proprio n., robot. Proprio Robotics, United States. Robots for building out, maintaining and decommissioning data centers.

    Push the Talking Trash Can n., robot. United States. Radio controlled talking trash can robot that toured Tomorrowland at several Disney parks and still patrols Hong Kong Disneyland daily. In service.

    Q

    Qoobo n., robot. Yukai Engineering, Japan, 2018. Tailed cushion robot that wags its tail when stroked, made as a pet substitute. In production.

    R

    RB5X n., robot. RB Robot Corporation, United States, 1983. Personal robot from 1983 produced by RB Robot Corporation, a company based in Golden, Colorado.

    Reactorsaurus n., robot. Robot built to dismantle the disused Dounreay nuclear reactor in Caithness, where neutron activation had made conditions too hazardous for human workers.

    Robin n., robot. University of Kaiserslautern, Germany. Research robot for studying interaction between robots and humans, developed at the University of Kaiserslautern in Germany.

    Robin the Robot n., robot. Armenia. Robot developed in Armenia whose behaviour is driven by artificial intelligence software.

    RoboBee n., robot. Harvard University. Insect sized robot from Harvard University that flies, partly untethered, on wings flapped by artificial muscles. Research platform.

    Roboboa n., snake robot. WowWee. Robotic snake made by WowWee with four angled body sections that rotate against one another so it can coil up.

    Robop n., robot. Robop Ltd, United Kingdom, 2001. Robotic peregrine falcon from the British firm Robop Ltd that deters pest birds by flapping its wings, turning its head and sounding a falcon's call.

    Robopanda n., toy robot. WowWee. Interactive robotic companion toy from WowWee that loads personalities, stories and songs from swappable cartridges and reacts to touch and sound.

    Robosaurus n., robot. Doug Malewicki, 1989. Giant driver operated robot, built in 1989 by inventor Doug Malewicki, that changes from a 48 foot semi trailer into a Tyrannosaurus rex that devours cars at shows. In service.

    Robosquirrel n., robot. University of California, Davis and San Diego State University, United States. Robotic squirrel built in several versions by researchers at the University of California, Davis and San Diego State University to study how predator and prey signals evolve. Research platform.

    Robostrider n., robot. United States. Self propelled robot that copies the way water striders move so it can skim across the surface of water, developed in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

    Robotis Bioloid n., robot kit. Robotis, South Korea. Robot kit for hobbyists and education from the South Korean company Robotis, using modular servo units that combine into wheeled, legged or humanoid machines.

    Roybi n., robot. Roybi Robot. Companion robot that relies on artificial intelligence software, made by the company Roybi Robot.

    RuBot II n., robot. Ireland. Rubik's Cube solving robot built by Irish roboticist and inventor Pete Redmond that entered the Guinness Book of World Records as the fastest machine of its kind.

    S

    s-bot n., robot. EPFL, Switzerland, 2004. Small differential wheeled mobile robot, 15 cm in size, developed at the EPFL in Lausanne between 2001 and 2004 for swarm robotics research in the Swarm bots project. Research platform.

    Sarcoman n., robot. Sarcos. Show robot from Sarcos that performs either under the live control of a human operator or from programmed computer routines in automated shows.

    Senster n., robot. Edward Ihnatowicz, 1968. Robotic artwork by Edward Ihnatowicz that Philips commissioned for the Evoluon in Eindhoven, the Netherlands, where it was exhibited from 1970 until its dismantling in 1974.

    Silver Swan n., automaton. James Cox, United Kingdom, 1773. Clockwork swan automaton made by James Cox in the 18th century, now displayed at the Bowes Museum in Barnard Castle, County Durham.

    Somnox Sleep Robot n., robot. Somnox. Capsule shaped robot that rises and falls in a fixed breathing rhythm, meant to help users fall asleep by matching their own breathing to it.

    SORA-Q Flagship Model n., toy robot. Tomy, Japan, 2023. Radio controlled mobile toy robot released in 2023 by the Japanese toy company Tomy as the flagship model bearing the SORA-Q name.

    SPHERES n., robot. Payload Systems, Inc., United States, 1999. Set of free flying robots developed by MIT's Space Systems Laboratory for NASA and the US military to test formation flight, docking and autonomy algorithms aboard the International Space Station.

    Squawkers McCaw n., animatronic figure. 2007. Animatronic toy parrot from the Fur Real range, modelled on a blue and yellow macaw and made to behave like a real parrot.

    Squirrel Robot n., robot. LIST, France, 1990. Inspection robot built in France in 1990 by LIST to get into hazardous parts of nuclear plants and send back reports on conditions there.

    StackChan n., robot. M5Stack, China. Open source desktop robot sold as a product by the Chinese company M5Stack, with a mass of 187 g.

    T

    TekBots n., robot. Programmable teaching robots that a number of universities use to give students a grounding in the basics of computer and electrical engineering.

    TERMES n., robot. Harvard University, 2014. Team of termite inspired autonomous robots from Harvard University that build structures from blocks by following simple rules, with no central controller, first shown in 2014. Prototype.

    The Grand Elephant n., animatronic figure. La Machine, United Kingdom, 2007. Giant animatronic elephant built by the French theater company La Machine, about 12 m tall and 8 m wide, with a top speed of 1 km/h.

    The Trons n., robot. New Zealand, 2008. Robot band of four machines that play music by themselves, built from spare parts and electronics by Hamilton based musician Greg Locke.

    Theseus n., robot. Bell Labs, United States, 1950. Magnetic robotic mouse built at Bell Labs in the United States in 1950.

    Tico n., robot. Adele Robots, Spain. Spanish social robot made by Adele Robots to engage with people, chiefly at marketing events and in schools as a teacher's assistant.

    Tipu's Tiger n., automaton. India, 1790. Eighteenth century wooden automaton made for Tipu Sultan of Mysore, depicting a tiger attacking a British man, with a pipe organ inside its body.

    Tlaloc II-TC n., robot. Robot built to explore the tunnel found in 2003 beneath the Temple of the Feathered Serpent at Teotihuacan, the ancient site in central Mexico.

    Tobor n., toy robot. Schaper Toys, United States, 1978. Toy robot made in 1978 by Schaper Toys that responded to loud sounds or to a control box through the company's telesonic remote system.

    Tradinno n., animatronic figure. Germany. Huge animatronic dragon that walks and breathes fire, built to play the dragon in the Drachenstich play in Furth im Wald, Germany. In service.

    Treads Robot n., robot. True Robotics, United States. Educational robot from the American company True Robotics, intended for classroom use from kindergarten through grade 3.

    Tuesday n., robot. Tuesday Labs, United States. Home robot from a New York startup built to pick up and tidy rooms.

    Tux Droid n., robot. KYSOH, 2007. Cordless penguin robot styled after Tux, the mascot of Linux, that was manufactured by the company KYSOH and came out around 2007.

    V

    Vaucanson Flute Player n., automaton. Jacques de Vaucanson, 1738. Android automaton built by Jacques de Vaucanson that played a standard transverse flute of the period, imitating a human flautist; it was first exhibited in 1738.

    VertiGo n., robot. ETH Zurich and Disney Research Zurich, Switzerland. Remote controlled wall climbing robot on wheels, developed by ETH Zurich and Disney Research Zurich, that uses two fully tilting propellers to push itself onto and up walls. Research platform.

    VEX IQ n., robot kit. VEX Robotics, United States. Snap together plastic robotics kit used by schools and in the VEX IQ competitions. In production.

    W

    Walker Chess player n., automaton. Walker Brothers, United States. Purported chess playing automaton built in the 1820s by the Walker Brothers of Baltimore, Maryland, as a rival to the Turk, a widely known chess machine. Retired.

    Winbot n., robot. Ecovacs, China. Window cleaning robot from Ecovacs that holds itself to the glass by suction and wipes in a set pattern. In production.

    WowWee Alive Chimpanzee n., animatronic figure. WowWee. Animatronic chimpanzee head from the WowWee Alive division of WowWee that uses motors and sensors to copy a live chimpanzee's facial expressions.
