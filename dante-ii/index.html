    Dante II

    n., rover. Carnegie Mellon University, United States, 1994. Retired.

    Tethered eight legged volcano robot that descended more than 200 metres into the crater of Mount Spurr in Alaska in 1994.

    Measures and facts
    MakerCarnegie Mellon University
    CountryUnited States
    First shown1994
    StatusRetired
    Mass770 kg
    ActuationEight legs walking in two frames with a rappelling tether winch
    PowerGenerator power supplied through the tether
    SensorsStereo cameras, scanning laser rangefinder, gas sampling instruments
    AutonomyTeleoperated with local reflexes and onboard terrain mapping

    Dante II was a much stronger rebuild of Dante I, combining a frame walking eight leg gait with a rappelling tether winch and a scanning laser rangefinder for mapping its surroundings. In July 1994 it walked down into the crater of Mount Spurr, sampled high temperature volcanic gases and was operated partly from Pittsburgh over a satellite link. On the way out it toppled on a steep slope and had to be lifted clear by helicopter, but it had already proved remote robotic volcano science.

    Of note

    • Descended more than 200 metres into an active Alaskan volcano
    • Sampled volcanic gases that would have endangered a human team
    • Fell during the return climb and was recovered by helicopter
