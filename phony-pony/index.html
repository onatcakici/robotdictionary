    Quadrupeds

    Phony Pony

    n., quadruped. University of Southern California, United States, 1966. Retired.

    Early electrically driven quadruped from USC, generally recognised as the first walking machine in the United States controlled entirely by a computer.

    Measures and facts
    MakerUniversity of Southern California
    CountryUnited States
    First shown1966
    StatusRetired
    Degrees of freedom8
    ActuationElectric motors at the hip and knee of each leg
    PowerExternal electrical supply
    AutonomyFinite state digital gait controller

    Andrew Frank and Robert McGhee built the Phony Pony to test whether finite state logic could generate walking gaits. Each of its four legs had two powered joints, a hip and a knee, driven by electric motors, and a digital controller switched the legs through walk and trot patterns. It was slow and tethered to external electronics, but it established the state machine approach to gait generation that later legged robots refined.

    Of note

    • Walked and trotted under computer control without human balance corrections
    • The work led to Robert McGhee's later hexapod research at Ohio State University
