    Underwater and marine

    Benthic Rover II

    n., marine robot. Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute, United States, 2015. In service.

    Autonomous tracked seafloor rover that has monitored carbon cycling on the abyssal plain off California for years at a time.

    Measures and facts
    MakerMonterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2015
    StatusIn service
    Height1.5 m
    Length2.6 m
    ActuationPair of wide rubber tracks for short repositioning moves
    PowerBattery pack drawing about two watts on average
    SensorsRespirometer chambers, oxygen sensors, fluorescence and still cameras, temperature loggers
    AutonomyFully autonomous year long measurement cycles with no tether

    The rover is about the size of a small car, rated to 6,000 metres and normally working at 4,000 metres depth at Station M, some 225 kilometres off central California. It crawls on wide rubber tracks a few metres at a time, then lowers transparent respirometer chambers onto the mud for 48 hour measurements of oxygen use, and photographs the sediment surface. Drawing an average of about two watts, it stays down for roughly a year between recoveries, and its records revealed pulses of sinking phytoplankton reaching the seafloor that short research cruises had missed.

    Of note

    • Works unattended on the abyssal seafloor for about a year per deployment
    • Recorded large pulses of sinking food reaching the seafloor between 2015 and 2020
