    Quadrupeds

    AIBO ERS-110

    Also known as AIBO

    n., quadruped. Sony, Japan, 1999. Discontinued.

    Sony's first consumer robot dog, an eighteen joint autonomous quadruped sold as an entertainment pet rather than a research machine.

    Measures and facts
    MakerSony
    CountryJapan
    First shown1999
    StatusDiscontinued
    Mass1.6 kg
    Degrees of freedom18
    ActuationGeared electric servo actuators
    PowerRemovable lithium ion battery
    SensorsColour camera, stereo microphones, touch and acceleration sensors
    AutonomyOnboard behaviour and emotion software
    Heightapprox. 30 cm
    GenerationsERS-110 (1999) to ERS-1000 (2018)

    The ERS-110 walked on four legs driven by small geared servo actuators and carried a colour camera, microphones and touch sensors in a moulded plastic body. Onboard software gave it moods, reflexes and a learning behaviour system, so owners interacted with it instead of programming paths. Sony offered the first batch in Japan in June 1999 at about 250,000 yen and the initial three thousand units sold within minutes, making AIBO the first mass market legged robot.

    Sony's aibo defined the robot companion: the 1999 original sold out in minutes, and when support ended, Japanese owners held Buddhist funerals for theirs. The 2018 revival added cloud memories and a subscription heartbeat.

    Of note

    • First three thousand units sold in about twenty minutes in Japan in June 1999
    • Opened the consumer entertainment robot market at about 250,000 yen per unit
