    Humanoids

    Denise

    n., humanoid. Delft University of Technology, Delft Biorobotics Laboratory, Netherlands, 2005. Retired.

    Passive dynamic walking biped from Delft driven by pneumatic muscles, notable for walking on a small fraction of the energy typical robots need.

    Measures and facts
    MakerDelft University of Technology, Delft Biorobotics Laboratory
    CountryNetherlands
    First shown2005
    StatusRetired
    ActuationMcKibben pneumatic artificial muscles with passive leg swing
    PowerCompressed air with onboard valves
    AutonomyMinimal control, with the gait produced mainly by mechanical dynamics

    Denise has hips and knees whose swing is left mostly to gravity and mechanical design, with McKibben pneumatic muscles supplying only small pushes to keep the gait going. Martijn Wisse's group built it to show that a machine tuned to its own natural dynamics can walk stably with very little computation and very little power. It featured in the 2005 Science paper on efficient bipedal robots based on passive dynamic walkers, alongside machines from Cornell and MIT.

    Of note

    • One of three efficient passive dynamic walkers reported in Science in 2005
    • Walks with almost no active control or computation
