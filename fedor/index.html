    Humanoids

    FEDOR

    Also known as Final Experimental Demonstration Object Research, Skybot F-850

    n., humanoid. FPI and Android Technics, Russia, 2019. Retired.

    Russian anthropomorphic robot flown uncrewed to the International Space Station in 2019 to test teleoperated work in weightlessness.

    Measures and facts
    MakerFPI and Android Technics
    CountryRussia
    First shown2019
    StatusRetired
    Height1.82 m
    Mass106 kg
    Top speed1.11 m/s (4 km/h)
    Runtime1 h
    Actuation48 electric motors
    Sensors48 position sensors with gyroscopic body attitude sensing
    AutonomyCopy operator teleoperation with limited autonomous actions

    FEDOR trained with pistols in videos that alarmed the world, then flew to the ISS in a Soyuz commander's seat in 2019, tweeting complaints throughout its two week stay.

    The machine is 182 cm tall and weighs about 106 kg, with 48 motors and a matching set of position sensors, and it copies the movements of an operator wearing an exoskeleton suit as well as running some actions on its own. It launched on Soyuz MS-14 on 22 August 2019, docked with the Zvezda module on 27 August after a first attempt failed, and on 30 August it mated electrical connectors while weightless. It returned to Earth on 6 September 2019, and the FEDOR platform it derives from had been developed earlier for rescue work.

    Of note

    • First Russian humanoid robot flown to the International Space Station
    • Mated electrical connectors in weightlessness in August 2019
