    Rovers

    Yutu

    Also known as Jade Rabbit, Chang'e 3 rover

    n., rover. China Academy of Space Technology, China, 2013. Retired.

    China's first lunar rover, landed in Mare Imbrium by Chang'e 3, which lost mobility after one lunar day but kept transmitting for years.

    Measures and facts
    MakerChina Academy of Space Technology
    CountryChina
    First shown2013
    StatusRetired
    Mass120 kg
    Distance driven0.114 km
    ActuationSix wheel rocker bogie drive with four corner steering
    PowerTwo folding solar panels with radioisotope heater units
    SensorsPanoramic and hazard cameras, lunar penetrating radar, infrared imaging spectrometer, alpha particle X ray spectrometer
    AutonomyTeleoperated with onboard hazard detection
    Length1.5 m

    Yutu drove off the Chang'e 3 lander on 15 December 2013, part of the first soft landing on the Moon since 1976. Solar panels that folded over the body for the lunar night powered six wheels, a lunar penetrating radar, an infrared imaging spectrometer and an X ray spectrometer. A control fault in January 2014 left it unable to move or stow its panels, yet it kept sending data until Chinese officials retired it in August 2016, having driven 114 metres.

    Of note

    • First Chinese rover on the Moon
    • Its radar revealed at least nine distinct subsurface layers
    • Kept working more than 31 months despite failing after 114 metres
