    Rovers

    Zhurong

    Also known as Tianwen-1 rover

    n., rover. China Academy of Space Technology, China, 2020. Lost.

    China's first Mars rover, which explored southern Utopia Planitia for a year and found evidence of water altered soils and buried shorelines.

    Zhurong rode the Tianwen-1 orbiter to Mars and landed in Utopia Planitia in May 2021, making China the second country to operate a rover on the Martian surface. Four foldable solar panels powered a six wheel active suspension that could lift a wheel or crab sideways, with ground penetrating radar, a laser induced breakdown spectrometer and a magnetometer. It drove 1,921 metres in about a year and reported hydrated minerals in young dune deposits and radar layering read as ancient coastal sediments, then entered hibernation in May 2022 and never responded again.

    Of note

    • Made China the second nation to drive a rover on Mars
    • Travelled 1,921 metres before hibernating in May 2022
    • Radar data supported the case for ancient shoreline deposits
