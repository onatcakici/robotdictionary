MIT Quadruped

Also known as MIT Leg Laboratory Quadruped

Hydraulic running quadruped from Marc Raibert's MIT Leg Laboratory that trotted, paced and bounded using a decomposed balance controller.

MakerMIT Leg Laboratory
CountryUnited States
First shown1984
StatusRetired
ActuationHydraulic telescoping legs
PowerOff-board hydraulic supply through a tether
AutonomyOff-board computer running decomposed balance control

The machine had four telescoping legs with hydraulic actuators and ran on a tether that supplied hydraulic power and computing. Control was split into separate parts for hopping height, forward speed and body attitude, an approach Raibert had already proven on one-legged hoppers. It ran on a treadmill and an indoor track and switched between trot, pace and bound, results that underpinned the dynamic quadrupeds built decades later.

Of note

  • Demonstrated trot, pace and bound gaits with active balance on a machine that ran rather than walked
  • Marc Raibert, who led the work, founded Boston Dynamics in 1992

Maker: www.mit.edu
