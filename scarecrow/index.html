    Rovers

    Scarecrow

    Also known as MSL mobility test rover

    n., rover. NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, United States, 2010. Research platform.

    Stripped down mobility twin of Curiosity built at reduced mass so its Earth weight matches the flight rover's weight on Mars.

    Measures and facts
    MakerNASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2010
    StatusResearch platform
    ActuationSix wheel rocker bogie drive matching the flight rover
    PowerExternal umbilical or battery power during tests
    SensorsInstrumented wheels with motion tracking during trials
    AutonomyManually commanded during mobility trials

    Scarecrow has Curiosity's suspension, wheels and drive actuators but no onboard computer, arm or instruments, which is where its nickname comes from. Because Martian gravity is about 38 percent of Earth's, building the testbed at roughly a third of Curiosity's mass makes its wheel loads on Earth match those of the flight rover on Mars. JPL drives it in the Mars Yard and on the Dumont dunes in California to check slopes, sand traps and wheel wear before commanding the real rover.

    Of note

    • Mass tuned so Earth tests reproduce Martian wheel loading
    • Used to test escape routes from soft sand before commanding Curiosity
