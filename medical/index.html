    Section VI

    Medical and surgical

    Surgical systems, rehabilitation robots and exoskeletons for patients. 49 entries.

    ABCDEFGHIKLMNPRSTVZ

    A

    AESOP n., medical robot. Computer Motion, United States, 1994. Robotic arm that held and moved the endoscope camera during keyhole surgery, the first surgical robot cleared by the US Food and Drug Administration. Retired.

    Andromeda n., medical robot. Andromeda Surgical, United States. Surgical robot developed to carry out parts of procedures autonomously rather than only extending a surgeon's hands.

    Atalante n., exoskeleton. Wandercraft, France. Self balancing exoskeleton from Wandercraft that lets people with paralysis walk without crutches. In production.

    B

    Berkeley Lower Extremity Exoskeleton n., exoskeleton. Berkeley Robotics and Human Engineering Laboratory, United States, 2004. Lower body powered exoskeleton developed by the Berkeley Robotics and Human Engineering Laboratory at the University of California, Berkeley, with DARPA funding, to add strength to leg movements. Prototype.

    C

    CosmoBot n., medical robot. Prototype telerehabilitation robot intended for therapy with children who have conditions such as autism, Down syndrome, cerebral palsy or muscular dystrophy. Prototype.

    CyberKnife n., medical robot. Accuray, United States. Radiation treatment system that mounts a linear accelerator on a robot arm to aim beams at tumours from many angles. In production.

    D

    da Vinci 5 n., medical robot. Intuitive Surgical, United States, 2024. Fifth generation da Vinci system, cleared in the United States in 2024, adding force feedback instruments. In production.

    da Vinci SP n., medical robot. Intuitive Surgical, United States, 2018. Version of the da Vinci system that passes a camera and three instruments through a single small incision. In production.

    da Vinci Surgical System n., medical robot. Intuitive Surgical, United States. Surgical robot system made by Intuitive Surgical for minimally invasive operations, including prostate removal, heart valve repair and kidney and gynaecological procedures. In production.

    da Vinci Xi n., medical robot. Intuitive Surgical, United States, 2014. Fourth generation da Vinci system with arms mounted on an overhead boom, so the robot can reach several parts of the abdomen without being moved. In production.

    Dexter n., medical robot. Distalmotion, Switzerland. Compact surgical robot from Distalmotion that hospitals can switch between robotic and ordinary keyhole surgery during a single operation. In production.

    E

    Ekso n., exoskeleton. Ekso Bionics, United States. Powered exoskeleton used in rehabilitation clinics to help people with stroke or spinal cord injury practise walking. In production.

    ExcelsiusGPS n., medical robot. Globus Medical, United States, 2017. Robotic navigation system that holds instruments on a planned path for placing screws in the spine. In production.

    F

    Flex Robotic System n., medical robot. Technion, Israel Institute of Technology, Israel, 2014. Medical snake robot for single port surgery, developed at the Technion in Israel and dating from 2014.

    G

    GD01 n., exoskeleton. Unitree Robotics, China. Large powered exoskeleton made by the Chinese company Unitree Robotics, standing 2.7 m tall with a mass of 500 kg.

    Guardian XO n., exoskeleton. Sarcos, United States. Battery powered full body exoskeleton from Sarcos built to let workers lift heavy loads repeatedly.

    H

    HAL n., exoskeleton. University of Tsukuba and Cyberdyne Inc., Japan, 2004. Powered exoskeleton suit with a soft body, known in full as the Hybrid Assistive Limb, built by Japan's University of Tsukuba and Cyberdyne to boost its wearers' physical abilities.

    Hardiman n., exoskeleton. General Electric, 1965. Early powered exoskeleton from General Electric, meant to give its wearer the strength to raise 1500 pounds (680 kg) with little effort. Retired.

    Hugo RAS n., medical robot. Medtronic, United States, 2021. Modular robotic surgery system from Medtronic with an open surgeon console, first used on a patient in Chile in 2021. In production.

    Human Universal Load Carrier n., exoskeleton. Ekso Bionics and Berkeley Robotics and Human Engineering Laboratory, United States, 2009. Untethered hydraulic exoskeleton developed for soldiers by H. Kazerooni's team at Ekso Bionics, intended to help wearers carry loads of up to 200 pounds. Prototype.

    I

    Indego n., exoskeleton. Parker Hannifin, United States. Lightweight powered hip and knee exoskeleton developed at Vanderbilt University and sold by Parker Hannifin.

    Ion n., medical robot. Intuitive Surgical, United States, 2019. Robotic system from Intuitive that steers a thin flexible catheter deep into the lungs to take biopsies of small nodules. In production.

    K

    KINARM n., medical robot. BKIN Technologies, Canada. Interactive robotic device that lets researchers measure how the brain handles sensing, movement and thinking through upper limb tasks, made by BKIN Technologies of Kingston, Ontario.

    L

    Lokomat n., medical robot. Hocoma, Switzerland. Robotic gait trainer that moves a patient's legs over a treadmill while part of their body weight is supported by a harness. In production.

    M

    Maestro n., medical robot. Moon Surgical, France. Pair of robotic arms from Moon Surgical that hold the camera and retract tissue for the surgeon during keyhole surgery. In production.

    Mako n., medical robot. Stryker, United States, 2006. Robotic arm system that guides surgeons cutting bone for knee and hip replacements according to a plan made from a CT scan. In production.

    Mazor X n., medical robot. Mazor Robotics, Israel, 2016. Robotic guidance system for spine surgery from Mazor Robotics, which Medtronic acquired in 2018. In production.

    Michelangelo Hand n., medical robot. Ottobock and Advanced Arm Dynamics, Germany, 2010. Robotic prosthetic hand with full articulation made by the German company Ottobock with its American partner Advanced Arm Dynamics, the first prosthesis with an electronically driven thumb. In production.

    MIT-Manus n., medical robot. MIT, United States. Arm rehabilitation robot from MIT that guides a patient's hand through reaching exercises after stroke, later sold under the InMotion name. Retired.

    Monarch n., medical robot. Auris Health, United States, 2018. Robotic platform for bronchoscopy developed by Auris Health, which Johnson and Johnson acquired in 2019. In production.

    Morphle n., medical robot. Morphle Labs, India. Robotic microscopes from Bengaluru that automate slide scanning for cancer diagnostics.

    N

    NeuroArm n., medical robot. Research robot for neurosurgery, the first image guided surgical robot compatible with magnetic resonance imaging that can carry out both microsurgery and stereotaxy. Research platform.

    P

    Perceptive n., medical robot. Perceptive Technologies, United States. Robotic system that pairs a new dental imaging method with automated tooth cutting, such as preparing a tooth for a crown.

    R

    Revolve n., medical robot. Revolve Surgical, Canada. Compact surgical robot designed to fit operating rooms outside hospitals, where many abdominal procedures take place.

    ReWalk n., exoskeleton. ReWalk Robotics, Israel, 2011. Commercial powered exoskeleton that lets people with paraplegia stand, walk and climb stairs, designed in Yokneam, Israel, by Amit Goffer. In production.

    ROBODOC n., medical robot. Integrated Surgical Systems, United States, 1992. Surgical robot that milled the cavity in the thigh bone for hip implants, first used on a patient in 1992. Retired.

    ROSA n., medical robot. Zimmer Biomet, France. Robotic surgical assistant first developed by Medtech in France for brain and spine procedures, later used for knee replacement. In production.

    S

    Sarcos-Raytheon XOS n., exoskeleton. Sarcos, United States. Powered exoskeleton developed by Sarcos in the United States, a wearable robotic frame that moves in step with the person inside it.

    Senhance n., medical robot. Asensus Surgical, United States, 2017. Laparoscopic surgical robot with eye tracking camera control and haptic feedback for the surgeon. In production.

    Sensei X n., medical robot. Medical robot that helps physicians guide a catheter, a small flexible tube, during complex minimally invasive operations.

    Sina Robotic Surgical System n., medical robot. Sina Robotics and Medical Innovators. Surgical robot for minimally invasive procedures made by Sina Robotics and Medical Innovators, used for prostatectomies and a growing range of other operations. In service.

    SOFIE n., medical robot. Eindhoven University of Technology, Netherlands. Surgical robot developed at Eindhoven University of Technology as part of Linda van den Bedem's doctoral thesis, the first such robot to give the surgeon force feedback. Research platform.

    T

    Trexo n., exoskeleton. Trexo Robotics, Canada. Powered walking exoskeleton that lets children with conditions such as cerebral palsy take steps, often for the first time.

    V

    Vanderbilt exoskeleton n., exoskeleton. Vanderbilt University, United States. Powered exoskeleton from Vanderbilt University's Center for Intelligent Mechatronics, sold as Indego, meant to help people affected by paraplegia, stroke or other forms of paralysis walk on their own.

    VELYS Robotic-Assisted Solution n., medical robot. DePuy, 2021. Orthopedic surgical robot introduced by DePuy in 2021 to assist surgeons with total knee replacement operations.

    Versius n., medical robot. CMR Surgical, United Kingdom, 2019. Modular surgical robot from CMR Surgical whose arms sit on separate carts so they can be arranged around the operating table. In production.

    Z

    Zeta Surgical n., medical robot. ZETA SURGICAL, United States. Image guidance and robotics platform that uses computer vision to register a patient in minutes for neurosurgical and transcranial magnetic stimulation procedures.

    ZEUS n., medical robot. Computer Motion, United States, 1998. Surgical robot with arms controlled from a separate console, withdrawn after its maker merged with Intuitive Surgical in 2003. Retired.

    ZEUS Robotic Surgical System n., medical robot. Intuitive Surgical, United States. Surgical robot made by Computer Motion that had three remotely controlled arms, won FDA clearance in 2001 and was dropped in 2003 after the merger with Intuitive Surgical. Discontinued.
