    Quadrupeds

    LittleDog

    n., quadruped. Boston Dynamics, United States, 2005. Retired.

    Small electric quadruped supplied to university teams as the shared hardware of the DARPA Learning Locomotion programme.

    Measures and facts
    MakerBoston Dynamics
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2005
    StatusRetired
    Degrees of freedom12
    ActuationElectric geared motors, three joints per leg
    PowerOnboard lithium polymer battery
    SensorsJoint encoders, inertial sensing, external motion capture
    AutonomyOff board planning and control over a wireless link

    LittleDog was deliberately simple and identical across teams so that research groups could compare footstep planning and control algorithms on the same machine. Each of its four legs had three electric joints with position sensing, and the robot walked over modular rock fields while an external motion capture system tracked it. Teams from Carnegie Mellon, MIT, Stanford, the University of Pennsylvania and elsewhere used it to develop terrain aware footstep planning that fed into later legged robots.

    Of note

    • Common platform of the DARPA Learning Locomotion programme from 2006
    • Its rock field benchmark shaped modern footstep planning research
