    Humanoids

    MABEL

    n., humanoid. University of Michigan with Carnegie Mellon University, United States, 2009. Retired.

    Boom constrained biped at Michigan that used springs in series with its motors to run at 3.06 m/s, a record for a robot with knees.

    Measures and facts
    MakerUniversity of Michigan with Carnegie Mellon University
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2009
    StatusRetired
    Mass65 kg
    Top speed3.06 m/s (11 km/h)
    ActuationElectric motors with cable differentials and series springs
    AutonomyHybrid zero dynamics feedback control, boom constrained to the sagittal plane

    MABEL weighs about 65 kg with most of its mass in the torso, and each hip and knee is driven by two motors through a cable differential with a spring in series, so the springs store and return energy during running. Jessy Grizzle's group controlled it with hybrid zero dynamics, an analytical method that stabilises a whole periodic gait instead of tracking a fixed trajectory. In August 2011 it ran at 3.06 m/s with a flight phase covering roughly a quarter of each stride, and it also walked over uneven ground and up a coarse ramp.

    Of note

    • Ran at 3.06 m/s in 2011, then the fastest for a knee equipped biped
    • Main experimental test bed for hybrid zero dynamics control
