    Quadrupeds

    SpaceBok

    n., quadruped. ETH Zurich Robotic Systems Lab, Switzerland, 2018. Retired.

    Jumping quadruped built by Swiss students to study legged movement in low gravity for lunar and asteroid exploration.

    Measures and facts
    MakerETH Zurich Robotic Systems Lab
    CountrySwitzerland
    First shown2018
    StatusRetired
    Height0.5 m
    Mass22 kg
    Degrees of freedom8
    ActuationElectric motors with spring energy storage in each leg
    PowerOnboard battery
    SensorsInertial measurement unit, joint encoders
    AutonomyOnboard jump and attitude control

    SpaceBok has four two joint legs with springs that store energy for repeated hops, plus an optional reaction wheel for controlling attitude while airborne. Started as a student project at ETH Zurich and ZHAW, it was tested on gantries and tilted planes that simulate lunar and asteroid gravity, including at the European Space Agency's orbital robotics laboratory. The work showed that a legged robot can travel by bounding in low gravity and reorient itself in flight like a spacecraft.

    Of note

    • Tested in simulated lunar and microgravity conditions at the European Space Agency's ESTEC laboratory
    • Used a reaction wheel to control body orientation during long jumps
