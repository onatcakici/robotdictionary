    Humanoids

    Cog

    n., humanoid. MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, United States, 1993. Retired.

    Upper body humanoid at MIT built to test the argument that human like intelligence has to be learned through embodied social interaction.

    Measures and facts
    MakerMIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown1993
    StatusRetired
    ActuationElectric series elastic actuators in the arms
    SensorsSaccading camera eyes, microphones, inertial sensing, joint position and force sensing
    AutonomyBehaviour based architecture learning from interaction with people

    Cog had a movable torso, arms, neck and saccading camera eyes, with compliant series elastic arm actuators so it could safely make contact with people and objects. Rodney Brooks and Lynn Andrea Stein's group used it through the 1990s for experiments in reaching, rhythmic arm motion, joint attention, face tracking and imitation rather than for preplanned industrial tasks. The project ran until 2003 and the robot now sits in the MIT Museum, having shaped a generation of work on embodied and developmental robotics.

    Of note

    • Central platform for the embodied intelligence argument at MIT
    • Retired in 2003 and now held by the MIT Museum
