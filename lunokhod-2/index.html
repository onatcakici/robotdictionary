    Rovers

    Lunokhod 2

    Also known as Luna 21 rover

    n., rover. NPO Lavochkin and VNII Transmash, Soviet Union, 1973. Retired.

    Second Soviet lunar rover, delivered by Luna 21 to Le Monnier crater, which drove roughly 39 kilometres in under four months.

    Measures and facts
    MakerNPO Lavochkin and VNII Transmash
    CountrySoviet Union
    First shown1973
    StatusRetired
    Mass840 kg
    Distance driven39 km
    ActuationEight independently driven wheels with individual motors and brakes
    PowerLid mounted solar panel with polonium 210 radioisotope heater
    SensorsThree television cameras, panoramic telephotometers, boom mounted magnetometer, photodetector, solar X ray experiment, laser retroreflector
    AutonomyTeleoperated from Earth using television guidance

    Height135 cm

    Width160 cm

    Lunokhod 2 landed on 15 January 1973 and explored the boundary between Le Monnier crater and the Taurus mountains, driving faster than its predecessor thanks to a third television camera and an improved navigation system. Power came from a lid mounted solar panel with a polonium 210 heater for the lunar night, turning the same eight wheel chassis. It surveyed the local magnetic field and a nearby rille before its open lid scooped dust onto the radiator in May 1973, causing the rover to overheat.

    Of note

    • Held the off world driving record for four decades
    • Agreed traverse of about 39 km, re estimated near 42 km from orbital imagery
    • Carried a French laser retroreflector still used for ranging
