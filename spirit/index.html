    Rovers

    Spirit

    Also known as MER-A

    n., rover. NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, United States, 2003. Retired.

    Solar powered Mars Exploration Rover that worked in Gusev crater for six years and found mineral evidence of ancient hot water.

    Measures and facts
    MakerNASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2003
    StatusRetired
    Height1.5 m
    Mass185 kg
    Distance driven7.73 km
    ActuationSix wheel rocker bogie drive with four corner steering
    PowerSolar arrays with rechargeable lithium ion batteries
    SensorsPanoramic and navigation cameras, microscopic imager, alpha particle X ray and Moessbauer spectrometers, thermal emission spectrometer
    AutonomyGround planned drives with onboard hazard avoidance and visual odometry
    Length141 cm
    Width122 cm

    Spirit landed in Gusev crater on 4 January 2004 for a planned 90 sol mission and kept working for 2,208 sols. Solar arrays and lithium ion batteries drove six wheels on a rocker bogie chassis, with a camera mast, a robotic arm and spectrometers for geology. It climbed the Columbia Hills and found silica deposits and carbonates pointing to hot spring or steam vent activity, then became embedded in soft sand at a site called Troy in 2009 and fell silent in March 2010.

    Spirit landed with a ninety day plan and worked Mars for six years, climbing Husband Hill and outliving every expectation before soft sand and winter ended the mission in 2010.

    Of note

    • Operated 2,208 sols against a planned 90 sol mission
    • Found silica rich deposits indicating an ancient hydrothermal setting
    • Drove 7.73 km before becoming stuck in soft sand
