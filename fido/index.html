    Rovers

    FIDO

    Also known as Field Integrated Design and Operations rover

    n., rover. NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, United States, 1999. Retired.

    Terrestrial prototype rover used to rehearse Mars Exploration Rover science operations in desert field tests before the 2003 launches.

    Measures and facts
    MakerNASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown1999
    StatusRetired
    ActuationSix wheel rocker bogie drive with four corner steering
    PowerSolar panel with rechargeable batteries
    SensorsMast mounted panoramic and infrared imagers, microscopic imager, mini corer
    AutonomyRemote operations with autonomous navigation and instrument placement

    FIDO carried a mast with panoramic and infrared instruments plus an arm with a microscopic imager and a mini corer for rock sampling, closely mirroring the payload later flown on Spirit and Opportunity. Between 1999 and 2002 it worked at sites in Nevada, California and Arizona while science teams operated it blind from JPL, exactly as they would a Mars mission. Those campaigns trained many of the people who went on to run the flight rovers.

    Of note

    • Trained the science teams that later operated Spirit and Opportunity
    • Carried a mini corer to practise rock sampling in the field
