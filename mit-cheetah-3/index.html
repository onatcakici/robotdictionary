    Quadrupeds

    MIT Cheetah 3

    n., quadruped. MIT Biomimetic Robotics Laboratory, United States, 2018. Research platform.

    Forty kilogram electric quadruped that climbed cluttered stairs and jumped onto tables using only proprioception, with no cameras.

    Measures and facts
    MakerMIT Biomimetic Robotics Laboratory
    CountryUnited States
    First shown2018
    StatusResearch platform
    Mass41 kg
    Top speed3 m/s (10.8 km/h)
    Degrees of freedom12
    ActuationProprioceptive brushless motors with low gear ratio transmissions
    PowerOnboard battery pack
    SensorsInertial measurement unit and joint encoders only
    AutonomyModel predictive control with contact detection and no exteroceptive sensing

    Cheetah 3 kept the proprioceptive actuators of the earlier Cheetah machines and added greater joint range and a stiffer frame. Two algorithms did the work: a contact detection routine that decided when each foot had truly touched down, and a model predictive controller that recomputed leg forces every fifty milliseconds. Working blind from gyroscopes, accelerometers and joint sensors alone, it climbed a debris strewn staircase and leapt onto a desk about seventy six centimetres high.

    Of note

    • Climbed stairs littered with obstacles with no vision at all
    • Its control software became the basis of the open source stack used on Mini Cheetah
